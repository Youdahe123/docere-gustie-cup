A 2,000-Year-Old Problem

The best education has always been one-on-one.

1800s — The Industrial Age

We traded personalization for scale

💻

2000s — The Digital Age

New tools, same model

We added textbooks, then computers, then online platforms. But the core model never changed — one teacher, many students.

Every leap in education changed the tools.

But none of them changed the model.

For two thousand years, we've known what great teaching looks like.

We just haven't been able to scale it.

The 2 Sigma Problem  ·  Benjamin Bloom, 1984

Students who received one-on-one tutoring performed two standard deviations better than students in traditional classrooms.

That means an average tutored student outperformed 98% of students in a regular classroom.

Bloom proved that personal tutoring is dramatically more effective — but admitted there was no practical way to give every student a personal tutor. That was 40 years ago. Nobody has solved it.

Then came AI.

In November 2022, ChatGPT launched. Within two months — 100 million users. The fastest adoption of any technology in human history.

The Student Problem

AI remembers your name.
It still doesn't know your classroom.

84%
of high school students now use AI tools like ChatGPT for schoolwork. — College Board, 2025

Yes, ChatGPT now has a "memory" feature. It can store about 33 facts about you — your name, your preferences, a few conversation topics. But that's it.

It still can't connect to your classroom.

It doesn't know what courses you're enrolled in.

It doesn't know what assignment is due Thursday or what the rubric says.

It doesn't know what grade you got on your last test.

Its memory holds ~15 conversation summaries — older sessions quietly disappear.

What ChatGPT remembers

✓  Your name

✓  That you're a student

✓  A few recent conversation topics

What it still doesn't know

✗  Your courses, teacher, or classmates

✗  Your assignments, rubrics, or deadlines

✗  Your grades or quiz results

✗  Your syllabus or course materials

✗  What you struggled with 2 weeks ago

Students still have to manually provide all of this context — every time they need real help.

Introducing Docere.

Latin for "to teach." An AI tutor that connects to your classroom, builds a brain for every student, and gets smarter the more it teaches.

1

Connect to your classroom

Docere plugs directly into your school's institution-wide platform — the same place teachers already post assignments and grades. One click. Nothing new to learn.

2

Build an artificial brain

From day one, Docere starts building two brains: a classroom brain that understands the whole course, and a student brain for each learner — tracking what they know, what they struggle with, and how they learn best.

3

Teach, don't tell

Instead of handing students the answer, Docere asks them questions — guiding them to figure it out themselves, the way a great teacher would. Every response is grounded in their real coursework.

Get smarter every week

Docere measures whether its teaching actually helps. Strategies that improve grades grow stronger. Strategies that don't get replaced. The tutor evolves — and teachers see everything.

Our Research

Learning Agents that Learn

We're not just building a product. We're conducting IRB-level research to prove that an AI tutor can measurably improve its own teaching over time.

What We're Studying

Can an AI tutor learn to teach better? We're connecting tutoring session outcomes to real student grades to measure whether the AI's teaching strategies actually improve learning.

Self-improving teaching strategies. The system evaluates its own interactions, identifies what worked, and evolves its approach — then we measure if students perform better.

First of its kind. No existing study has shown a closed loop between AI tutoring, self-evaluation, strategy evolution, and measurable grade improvement.
